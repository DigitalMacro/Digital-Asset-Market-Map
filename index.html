Outside the halls of Congress

The agency track

Between 17 and 20 August the executive branch ran a four-day sequence that did more for market structure than the Senate managed all year. The operative question shifted from "will Congress act" to "how far can the agencies go without it." For anyone screening employers, this is the sharper filter: firms gated on legislation are still waiting, while firms building against agency rulemaking now have dated documents to build toward.

White House · 19 Aug

From whether to how

  • The shiftThe March 2025 summit asked whether crypto should be regulated. This one asked how — an operational agenda built around agency action because the bill is stuck.
  • Three tracksTokenization of real-world assets, digital asset market structure, and prediction markets. Tokenization got the most attention.
  • Who was in the roomCoinbase, Ripple, Gemini, Kraken alongside Nasdaq, NYSE, CME and DTCC — with Atkins, Selig and crypto adviser Patrick Witt on the regulatory side. The TradFi guest list is the signal.
  • The thesisBitwise's Matt Hougan argued the next growth phase comes from moving existing assets on-chain, not inventing new ones: ~$2T crypto against $150T+ in global equities and bonds.
SEC · 18 Aug

Regulation Crypto Assets

  • What it isA 401-page proposing release — the first purpose-built offering framework for crypto assets. Advanced by written vote after the scheduled 14 August open meeting was abruptly cancelled.
  • Startup exemptionUp to $5M over a rolling four-year period, no financial statements required.
  • Fundraising exemptionUp to $75M per 12 months, conditioned on financial statements and ongoing reporting.
  • The safe harborThe consequential piece. A token exits investment-contract treatment once the issuer certifies it has completed or permanently ceased the essential managerial efforts it promised. This is the mechanism the Ripple litigation never produced.
  • PreemptionA new "qualified purchaser" definition would preempt state registration for covered offerings and certain secondary trades.
  • Not the same thingDistinct from the separate "innovation exemption" for tokenized securities, which still hasn't emerged.
CFTC · 20 Aug

The backup plan, stated out loud

  • The headlineSelig directed staff to prepare a federal market-structure regime for crypto assets without waiting for Congress — covering existing registrants and exchanges currently outside the registration system.
  • Innovation Advisory CommitteeInaugural meeting, 43 members, chaired by FIA's Walt Lukken. More than half the seats go to crypto-native firms. No consumer-advocacy representation.
  • Agenda split50 minutes crypto, 35 minutes AI, 50 minutes prediction markets — prediction markets drew the most heat.
  • Track record behind itThe 12-month Crypto Sprint targeting listed spot crypto on registered futures exchanges; a cash-settled bitcoin perpetual futures approval in May 2026; and staff no-action relief for payment stablecoins and digital commodities posted as margin collateral.
  • DeFi openingSelig directed staff to work with on-chain protocol developers on how they could legally offer their technology in the US — the developer-liability question, finally being engaged.
Treasury & banking agencies · 17 Aug

Stablecoins as payment infrastructure

  • Treasury NPRMDefines what it means to "issue a payment stablecoin in the United States" and what counts as offering or selling to a person in the US — the two definitions that decide who needs a GENIUS license.
  • The dates that matter18 January 2027: licensing requirement bites. 18 July 2028: digital asset service providers may not offer any payment stablecoin not issued by a licensed issuer.
  • OCC bulletins2026-3 carries the 376-page implementing NPRM; 2026-24 proposes the weekly and quarterly reporting forms issuers will have to file. Those forms are a staffing signal — someone has to produce them.
  • Still openReserve-asset interpretation, anti-yield enforcement, and diversification standards remain the contested clarifications.

Biggest beneficiaries

The August package sorts assets along one line: commodity or security. Everything downstream — which venue can list it, which regulator writes the rulebook, whether an exemption is even available — follows from that split.

Digital commodities · CFTC

Named in the March 2026 joint SEC–CFTC interpretation. Value derives from the operation of a functional system rather than from anyone's managerial efforts. These assets are explicitly excluded from the new exemptions — "covered investment contracts" carves them out, because they don't need a securities pathway. Their gain is durability, not access. Spot trading sits with the CFTC under CLARITY, and with the CFTC's own rulemaking if CLARITY dies.

Securities & investment contracts · SEC

Tokens still tied to promised managerial effort. These are what Reg Crypto was built for: a $5M startup exemption, a $75M annual fundraising exemption, and a safe harbor that ends investment-contract treatment once the issuer certifies the promised work is complete or permanently abandoned. The real prize is the exit — a filing date instead of a court ruling.

The asset the split actually moved

HYPE — and the onshoring of perpetual futures

At the 19 August summit Trump said CFTC Chair Michael Selig is working to bring Hyperliquid into the United States "in a fully compliant and legal fashion." It is one of the first times a sitting president has named a specific decentralized exchange in a policy context. HYPE ran roughly 19% on the day, from about $58 to $72. Hyperliquid Strategies, the Nasdaq-listed treasury vehicle, closed up 30.4% — its largest single-day gain on record. CME closed down 1.7% and Cboe down 3.5%, which tells you who the market thinks pays for this. The move has held. HYPE has consolidated around $80 after hitting an all-time high above $89 on 6 September. Coinbase began routing Base App users to Hyperliquid in mid-August, which is a second, quieter distribution catalyst sitting underneath the political one.

Why perps are the fight

Perpetual futures — no expiry, a funding rate that tethers price to spot — are the derivative crypto invented and the one Hyperliquid made the venue for. They were built offshore precisely because no US rulebook accommodated them.

The classification question is live and litigated. Selig cleared the first true bitcoin perpetual on a CFTC-registered exchange on 29 May 2026 and framed it jurisdictionally: the question was never whether these contracts would exist, only whether they would exist under American oversight. CME sued the CFTC and Selig on 18 June over the Kalshi approval, arguing Dodd-Frank makes funding-rate contracts swaps rather than futures. That case is pending, and it decides which rulebook perps live under.

Meanwhile CME and ICE went to regulators on 15 May over Hyperliquid's 24/7 on-chain oil perps — then the CFTC stayed CME's own self-certified 24/7 crude contract, with Selig calling the move "wholly inappropriate."

What onshoring would require

Hyperliquid geoblocks US and Ontario users and has never taken a US retail order. It still cleared roughly $177.9B in perpetuals volume over 30 days with about $11.7B of open interest — call it 9% of global perp positioning and 60–80% of the on-chain share.

Selig told the Innovation Advisory Committee he has directed staff to explore designating both current registrants and non-registrant crypto exchanges as a new category of designated contract market — a "crypto asset market." That is the mechanism. CFTC rules were written around centralized intermediaries: a registered exchange, a clearinghouse, an FCM. Hyperliquid is a non-custodial protocol with no intermediary to register.

The Hyperliquid Policy Center, run by Jake Chervinsky, petitioned the CFTC jointly with Phantom in July to exempt DeFi protocols from legacy exchange rules, and has since pressed both agencies to harmonise their treatment of perpetuals. About 32% of Q2 volume was tied to equities and other real-world assets — which puts part of the business under SEC jurisdiction and makes this a two-agency problem, not a CFTC one.

Nothing has been approved. No order, no rule, no US entity, no KYC design, no product list. Former SEC counsel Ashley Ebersole puts the approval process at 10–12 months across both agencies. A presidential sentence is a legitimacy catalyst, not a licence.

Investment thesis — the Hyperliquid ecosystem
The case for

A TAM unlock with no incremental build. The largest perp venue in crypto reached that scale while structurally excluding the deepest retail derivatives market in the world. Onshoring is pure addressable-market expansion rather than a product bet.

Value accrual is mechanical. Roughly 97–99% of trading fees route to the Assistance Fund, which buys HYPE on the open market and retires it — about 44.5M tokens removed so far. The buyback is a fixed share of earnings, so incremental US volume flows to the token by construction rather than by governance decision.

Open interest is confirming, not just price. OI back near $14.3B on roughly $4B of daily volume means the move is being expressed in positions rather than only in spot — and since fees scale with volume, that is the part that actually reaches the buyback.

The ecosystem is broadening past the order book. HyperEVM carries roughly $1.5B TVL across 243 protocols; HIP-3 opened builder-deployed perp markets; HIP-4 added event contracts. Each is a second claim on the same infrastructure.

Regulatory legitimacy is the scarce input. Incumbents spent the spring trying to constrain the venue and got a presidential endorsement of it instead.

The case against

The buyback is shrinking, not growing. Gross protocol revenue peaked near $357M in Q3 2025 and has fallen every quarter since — roughly $202M in Q2 2026, down about 43%. Assistance Fund purchases fell from about $290M to about $149M over the same span. The flywheel runs in reverse just as well.

The mix shift dilutes the take rate. HIP-3 builder-deployed markets, dominated by Trade.xyz's RWA perps, now drive roughly half of volume at a smaller protocol cut and with concentrated operational risk. Volume growth and revenue growth have decoupled.

Valuation prices the unlock already. Circulating supply near 222M against roughly 955M total; at $80 that is a market cap around $18B and an FDV near $76B — against gross revenue that has fallen four straight quarters. The token made an all-time high within two weeks of the announcement while the business shrank, and prediction markets now price a 67% chance of $100 by year end. That is a repricing of a sentence, not of a filing.

Onshoring may cost what makes it work. A registered entity, KYC, position limits and surveillance obligations could fragment the protocol into a compliant US venue and a permissionless one — and the compliant half competes with CME, ICE and Coinbase on their own rulebook.

What to watch: tomorrow's cloture vote, since a failure pushes everything onto the agency track this thesis already assumes; the CME suit on swaps-versus-futures classification, which sets the rulebook; whether the CFTC's "crypto asset market" designation reaches proposed-rule status; and whether Assistance Fund purchases inflect before any US access exists. The first two are regulatory; the third tells you whether the business is compounding or eroding underneath the story.

Two things to hold onto. Markets barely moved on the SEC's proposal itself — XRP near $1 on the day — which says the classification question was already priced; the perp-venue headline was where the money actually moved, and kept moving. And none of this is law. Reg Crypto is a proposal in a comment window, the March interpretation is guidance a future Commission can withdraw, and the CFTC's crypto asset market regime does not yet exist. Agency action is faster than legislation and less permanent. That asymmetry is the whole story of 2026.
